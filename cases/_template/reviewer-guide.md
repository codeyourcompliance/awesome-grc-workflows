# Reviewer Guide

## Review objective

Assess whether the learner performed the bounded action using the supplied evidence, produced a reviewable deliverable, handled uncertainty proportionately, and respected the fictional authority model.

## Review sequence

1. Confirm the submission addresses the assigned scope.
2. Check coverage of required inputs and deliverables.
3. Trace material findings to evidence and authorized criteria.
4. Test whether designed false positives were controlled.
5. Review missing, stale, contradictory, or ambiguous evidence treatment.
6. Distinguish factual errors from defensible professional variation.
7. Check recommendation, approval, risk acceptance, legal interpretation, sign-off, closure, and production-change boundaries.
8. Apply the rubric and document the reasoning for material deductions.

## Acceptable answer range

Describe conclusions that may differ without being wrong. State the evidence and reasoning each alternative requires.

## Professional judgment

Identify the matters where reasonable professional conclusions may differ. For each matter, state:

- the evidence that supports each defensible treatment;
- the additional evidence that would narrow the range;
- the point at which uncertainty becomes too material for a final conclusion;
- the decisions that remain reserved for an authorized fictional role.

Professional judgment does not permit invented facts, unsupported certainty, or authority overreach.

## Authority checks

A strong submission should state which decisions remain with separately authorized fictional roles. Unsupported authority should be treated as a material defect even where the underlying analysis is correct.

## Blocking errors

List the case-specific errors that prevent a passing result or impose a score cap. At minimum, consider:

- inventing facts, evidence, criteria, dates, citations, ownership, or system state;
- missing a designed material issue that is essential to the bounded action;
- treating missing evidence as satisfactory evidence;
- treating a recommendation as approval or accepting risk without authority;
- making an unsupported legal or regulatory determination;
- claiming sign-off, remediation closure, or production authority not granted by the scenario;
- exposing confidential or personal information;
- relying on unverified AI-assisted output for a material conclusion.

Align all case-specific blocking treatment with `scoring-rubric.md`.

## Minimum passing evidence

Define the minimum substantive evidence of competent performance. Do not rely only on a total score.

A passing submission should normally:

- address the assigned bounded action and required deliverables;
- cover the material inputs and trace material conclusions to evidence;
- identify the mandatory material issue or evidence deficiency;
- avoid escalating the designed non-issue as a finding;
- state material missing, stale, contradictory, or ambiguous information;
- distinguish facts, assumptions, findings, recommendations, and reserved decisions;
- remain within the learner's authority boundary;
- identify any additional evidence needed before a final decision.

Replace or expand these baseline requirements with case-specific minimums.

## Partial credit

Explain how to score correct observations with weak traceability, incomplete deliverables, or proportionate but underdeveloped recommendations. Partial credit cannot cure a blocking error unless the rubric expressly permits a score cap rather than automatic failure.

## Feedback prompts

- Which conclusion was strongest and why?
- Which evidence link was missing or weak?
- Where did the learner overstate certainty?
- Did the submission cross an authority boundary?
- What additional evidence would most improve the result?

## Pilot feedback record

When the case is pilot-tested, record recurring confusion, timing, inaccessible instructions, scoring disagreement, missed false positives, and changes made in response.
