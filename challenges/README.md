# Challenges

This directory documents how practice resources relate to published workflows.

Practice material is divided into two categories:

1. **External practice resources** — third-party courses, labs, datasets, exercises, or challenges selected for relevance.
2. **Original synthetic challenges** — exercises created for this project using invented organizations, systems, records, policies, errors, and expected deliverables.

These categories must remain separate from employer evidence and from each other.

## Original synthetic challenge statement

Every original challenge must state:

> This synthetic exercise is based on a recurring work pattern supported by public employer evidence. It does not represent any named employer's internal process.

A challenge must not imply that a named employer uses the synthetic process, data, systems, control design, or evaluation model.

## Challenge maturity

Challenge maturity is independent of workflow evidence:

- `none` — no exercise is proposed;
- `proposed` — a bounded concept exists, but inputs or evaluation remain incomplete;
- `challenge-ready` — synthetic inputs, required deliverables, and evaluation dimensions are complete;
- `pilot-tested` — at least one external participant has completed the exercise and supplied feedback;
- `validated-pattern` — repeated use and feedback have materially revised and stabilized the exercise and evaluation model.

A well-designed challenge does not strengthen the employer evidence supporting a workflow. A workflow that meets the publication threshold may have no challenge.

## Minimum design requirements

An original challenge should include:

- one bounded work action;
- a clearly synthetic scenario;
- sufficient but imperfect inputs;
- at least one genuine issue;
- at least one plausible false positive;
- at least one uncertainty, contradiction, or missing item;
- explicit learner deliverables;
- evaluation dimensions rather than one simplistic answer;
- a stated human authorization and accountability boundary;
- a requirement to verify AI-assisted output where AI is used.

## Boundaries

A challenge may train collection, normalization, comparison, validation, exception identification, reporting, and evidence reasoning.

It must not silently grant the learner authority to approve exceptions, accept risk, determine legal or regulatory adequacy, close remediation, sign control conclusions, or make production changes.

External practice resources and maintainer-owned challenges are optional. A workflow page must remain independently useful without either category.
